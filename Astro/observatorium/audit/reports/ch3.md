# Chapter 3 (Planets and lenses: transit 3.1, census 3.2, microlens 3.3): audit

## Summary
- All three missions can be finished through the UI by a student who has done chapters 1–2. None of them asks for a locked axis, variable or menu (the *needs* of all three are empty). But 3.1's unlock does nothing (B7), and 3.3 is hard to finish with the mouse alone.
- Worst bugs: (B1) a box selection also picks up points whose x value is missing (chart.js:454). In 3.2, 237 planets with no orbital period count as "hot Jupiters", so the result says 69% were found by transits instead of about 92%. (B8) For 64% of the transit planets, the "Planet mass" is a radius-based estimate, not a measurement. The census chart is built on it.
- Other bugs: stale χ² and a *What does it mean?* button that stays enabled after a switch of event; folding goes silently off after an axis change; several wrong numbers in the texts (Mercury ×13, 51 Peg ×10, t_E 30–40 d).
- Top 3 improvements: (1) fix B1 and B8, and let the 3.1 unlock open planet radius and density for 3.2; (2) a microlensing builder (M, D_L, v, b → t_E) plus the MACHO optical-depth exercise from Lecture 4, and a planet-anomaly event; (3) move the star-formation and IMF beats out of 3.2 into their own mission (IMF from Gaia 100 pc), and add a radial-velocity exercise. 3.2 currently teaches no Doppler measurement at all.
- OGLE: the microlensing photometry has no permission or credit notice anywhere a viewer sees it, only a collapsed line in the Data tab. Ask OGLE before publishing, or swap in public data (see "OGLE permission" below).

## Mission by mission

### 3.1 transit (A shadow crosses a star)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes.** Needs nothing; nothing it names is locked. *Kepler light curves*, *Star* HAT-P-7 / HAT-P-11, Fold checkbox, Trial period, Fine tune, Shift phase and the draggable "bottom of the dip" rule are all there. Played (t2_transit.js): tick Fold (label click); one mouse click on Trial period near 2.2 gives 2.2028 d "(dips stacked)"; drag the rule from 0.9985 to 0.9933 → done. No console errors.
- **Lecture** (5 beats): clear and short; the transit explainer (k, impact parameter, grazing V) is good. Missing from the slides: the transit-duration formula with the impact parameter b and the inclination (slide "זמן הטרנסיט … נוכל למצוא את b"; the game gives only T ≈ (P/π)(R★/a)); "transit plus RV gives density, rock or gas" gets one clause; systems with several transiting planets (slide). The explainer ignores limb darkening, though the explanation then asks the student to see it. There is no picture: a Kepler field image or the HD 209458 discovery curve would help.
- **Exercise**: the task never says how to find the period. A hint helps: "zoom in, read the time between two dips, or count the dips in 89 days". At 2P (4.41 d) the dips also stack (alternate transits) and the status says nothing. Add "try half". The folded dip is a narrow spike over two cycles, so "look at the rounded bottom (limb darkening)" needs a zoom the page does not do. Auto-zoom on the dip at done. Mouse resolution on Trial period is about 1.5% per pixel against a ±0.2% tolerance: the first click happened to land inside it; Fine tune makes it fair.
- **Explain & practice**: B2, B4. The prediction (2× wider → 4× deeper) is a good misconception check. One practice problem only: add a duration or an a-from-P problem (Tutorial 3 does both).
- **Views**: sky cues work (Kepler field, HAT-P-7 photo at 0.23°). The unlock is empty (B7).

### 3.2 census (Who finds which planets)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes.** x = Orbital period, y = Planet mass, colour = How it was found, all unlocked; *Orbit size a*, *Planet radius* and *Equilibrium temperature* are open too. Played (t3_census.js): box P 0.5–10 d, M 0.3–30 M_J → done, but with B1's wrong numbers. The thresholds are loose: a box P 0.3–100 d, M 0.1–50 M_J also passes (1,437 selected, median 4.3 d), and "the hot Jupiters" then include 100-day planets. Better: require that ≥ 80% of the selection has P < 10 d and M > 0.3 M_J.
- **Lecture** (8 beats): beats 1–4 are on topic (51 Peg, the Doppler formula, the bias explainer, direct imaging with the slide's 0.25″ / 0.05″ example). Beats 5–7 (ISM and Jeans mass, IMF, discs) are Lecture 3 §3.1 material wedged into a mission about detection bias. They are good, but they make this the longest lecture of the chapter and break its thread. The selection explainer puts a probability in % and a velocity in m/s on one "signal" axis, which confuses; use two panels, or one y-axis per curve.
- **Exercise**: selecting the hot-Jupiter clump shows that transits dominate it, but the prediction is about bias. Nothing in the exercise makes the student *see* the bias (e.g. colour by method and compare the share of each method in the hot-Jupiter box with the whole sample, or plot transit probability R★/a against a). The chart's mass axis is mostly estimates for transit planets (B8). No RV measurement anywhere in the chapter, even though RV is the first method on the slides and the whole of 51 Peg's story.
- **Explain & practice**: the four practice problems are right and well chosen. The result's "one observer in 8.8 / 1,118" is right. The explanation should state the slide's population result: about half of Sun-like stars have an Earth-to-Neptune planet within 1 AU, and the distribution is roughly flat in log M and log a.
- **Views**: the sky switches to the whole-sky map with planets coloured by method; the Kepler field and the microlensing planets toward the bulge stand out. Good. The legend box covers the top-right corner of the chart, where the imaging planets are.

### 3.3 microlens (A star brightens for a month)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes, but fiddly.** Needs nothing locked. *Microlensing events*, *Event* 2019-BLG-0148 and five sliders are there. Played (t4_microlens.js): one click per slider at values read off the chart (peak ≈ 8651, baseline 13.40, t_E ≈ 50 d, u₀ ≈ 0.35) gives χ²/N = 196. Reaching the target (8.2) took about 200 arrow-key nudges (final t_E = 60.8 d, u₀ = 0.279, f_s = 0.99, I₀ = 13.418; OGLE's fit: 58.2 d, 0.279). With the mouse the baseline slider moves 0.003 mag per pixel, so the last factor of 20 in χ² needs the keyboard, which the page never mentions. The target "χ²/N below 8.2" is 1.5 × the χ²/N of OGLE's own parameters (5.5): a student taught that a good fit has χ²/N ≈ 1 gets no explanation (the errors are underestimated in crowded fields). Add one line, and say "use the arrow keys for fine steps".
- **Lecture** (7 beats): strong. It has history, the 1919 plate, the Einstein radius with the HW3 numbers, the Horseshoe and the Cross, and a live two-image explainer, and its formulas are right. Missing from the slides: the deflection derivation, the weak-field and thin-lens assumptions, the strong-lensing list (magnified and sheared, shifted, multiple images, time delay), the lens equation β = θ − θ_E²/θ (HW4 Q2c), and A(u = 1) = 1.34, the threshold Lecture 4 uses to define an event. Lecture 4's MACHO argument (optical depth τ ≈ v²/c² ≈ 10⁻⁶, about 10 events at any time among 10⁷ LMC stars, t_E of half a year for 1 M☉ and a week for Jupiter masses, too few events seen) is one sentence here, with no numbers.
- **Exercise**: B9, B10, B13. One light curve, one fit; t_E is never turned into anything. HW3 Q2a asks the student to go from M, D_L, D_S, v, b to the light curve. Add the reverse step: "with D_L = 4 kpc and v = 150 km/s, what lens mass gives your t_E?" (≈ 1.6 M☉ for 58 d). That shows the degeneracy the explanation only names. HW3 Q2b (a planet's spike) is mentioned but never shown.
- **Explain & practice**: B5, B14. One practice problem only (A at u₀ = 0.1). Add the HW3 numbers (R_E, t_E) and the Lecture 4 MACHO count.
- **Views**: the sky cue to the bulge in Galactic coordinates and the event marker work. The beat "What surveys found" photograph is fine.
- **OGLE permission**: the light curve appears with only the dataset subtitle "OGLE-IV Early Warning System, 2019". The permission condition ("OGLE asks to be contacted before its EWS photometry is used in publications… ask OGLE before sharing this page publicly") appears only in the Data tab, inside a collapsed entry (logbook.js:251, from manifest.json), and in the README. Nothing under the chart, on the mission card, in the explanation or in "What really happened" says so. Before the page is published: (a) write to OGLE (A. Udalski) for permission. Once given, show under the chart and in "What really happened": "Photometry: OGLE-IV Early Warning System (Udalski 2003; Udalski, Szymański & Szymański 2015), courtesy of the OGLE team, used with permission". (b) Until then, a build switch that leaves `microlensing.csv` out of the public build, and either a light curve simulated from OGLE's published parameters (marked as simulated) or public data. Gaia DR3's microlensing catalogue (Wyrzykowski et al. 2023, A&A 674, A23) gives G, BP and RP light curves under Gaia's open licence, which would also let the student check "the same in every colour" (not verified: event choice and licence wording).

## Gaps against the slides
Lecture 3's main headings and how they are covered:
- **§3.1 star formation**: molecular clouds, the Jeans criterion and fragmentation, the free-fall time (~4×10⁴ yr), dissociation and ionisation energy sinks, a 60,000 K protostar of 0.3 AU, T Tauri stars, 10⁷–10⁸ yr to the main sequence. Covered: the Jeans mass and fragmentation (3.2 beat 5). Missing: the free-fall time, the energy argument and the protostar numbers.
- **IMF (Salpeter, HW3 Q1)**: covered as a live chart (3.2 beat 6). Missing: "most of the light from the few massive stars, most of the mass in small ones" as a calculation (HW3 Q1), and the link from O stars to HII regions (HW3 Q1e).
- **§3.2 HII regions**: Strömgren radius (~0.2 pc at 10⁴ cm⁻³), 10⁴ K, recombination lines, resonant scattering of Lyα, collisional excitation, forbidden lines and the [O III] doublet, "nebulium". Not covered in chapter 3 (no "Strömgren", "forbidden" or "nebulium" in lectures.js; lectures2.js mentions forbidden lines once, in chapter 9).
- **§3.3 ISM**: the phases, 21 cm hyperfine (mentioned only in passing in 3.2 beat 5), molecules (CO, OH, NH₃, alcohol), masers, dust extinction and reddening with the 2175 Å bump. Not covered.
- **§3.4 shocks, supernova remnants, cosmic rays** (10³–10⁴ km/s, keV gas, synchrotron, dN/dE ∝ E^−2.7): not covered.
- **Planets**: the planet/brown-dwarf boundary is missing. RV: the formula is present, but the slide's numbers are missing (Jupiter at 1 AU → 28 m/s, about 1 m/s precision today, about a thousand RV planets) and so is hot Jupiters' equilibrium temperature (the slide works T_eq at 0.05 AU). The *Equilibrium temperature* variable exists but nothing uses it. Transits: duration and impact parameter (above). Direct imaging: covered well, though the slide's contrast formula with albedo A and the coronagraph are only named. **Astrometry** and **pulsar timing** (the first exoplanets, 1991, around a millisecond pulsar) are missing; pulsar timing is in the dataset's methods but not in the lecture. Spectral signatures (transmission spectra) are missing.
- **Statistics**: each method samples its own corner of parameter space (covered). Missing: transit and Doppler surveys favour bright FGK stars while microlensing sees faint ones; half of Sun-like stars have Earth-to-Neptune planets within 1 AU; super-Earths; dN ∝ M^α a^β dlog M dlog a with α ≈ β ≈ 0.
- **Planet formation**: the snow line is covered (the slide gives 150 K and a 15 M⊕ core to accrete gas: numbers missing), as are the habitable zone and HL Tau. The Oort cloud is in chapter 4.
- **Microlensing / lensing history**: see 3.3 above. The slide's 1919 London News and New York Times headlines would be a nice touch.
- **Lecture 4, MACHOs**: optical depth and expected event counts and durations (missing, see 3.3), and why brown dwarfs and planets were good candidates until microlensing ruled them out.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
- **Microlensing builder (HW3 Q2a)**: sliders for M, D_L, D_S, v and b, with the Einstein ring and the source track drawn to scale (in AU and mas) and the light curve beside it. The student sees t_E and A_max change, and that M, D_L and v trade off (the degeneracy). It fixes "a bigger peak means a heavier lens". Easy: extend `X.lens` in explainers.js:385 with `A.einsteinRadius`.
- **Planet spike (HW3 Q2b)**: add a planet (q, s) to the lens explainer and show the short anomaly near the Einstein ring. A finite-source-free binary-lens solver is about 100 lines (or precompute a few caustic-crossing curves). Medium. Better still, ship a real planetary event as a second subset (e.g. OGLE-2005-BLG-390, also OGLE data, so permission applies).
- **MACHO counter (Lecture 4)**: the halo as MACHOs of mass m; the student sets m and sees the predicted number and duration of events toward the LMC over 5 years (100 events of half a year at 1 M☉, 2,600 of a week at 10⁻³ M☉) against the handful observed. It fixes "dark matter could just be dim stars". Easy: a new explainer with the slide's τ ≈ v²/c².
- **Transit explainer with limb darkening and duration**: a toggle for limb darkening (quadratic law) and a readout of T_dur(b) and the inclination. It fixes "the dip is flat and its depth is exactly k²". Easy: explainers.js:344.
- **Radial-velocity instrument**: a Doppler curve of 51 Peg b (published K = 56 m/s, P = 4.23 d) or HD 209458 with noise of 1–10 m/s; the student folds it and reads K to get M sin i. `INS.binary` already does this for stars (instruments.js), so reuse it with one curve.
- **Bias view in 3.2**: a "detectable by transit" shading of R★/a and a "Doppler K > 1 m/s" line over the P–M chart (reuse `chart.add({type:'curve'})`), so the student sees the empty corners are selection edges.

## Suggestions: new missions or exercises
- **IMF mission (HW3 Q1; DESIGN.md already lists it)**: count Gaia 100 pc main-sequence stars per log M bin, fit Salpeter's slope by hand, then compute where the mass and the light are. The data and the chart are already in 3.2 beat 6. Move beats 5–6 there and shorten 3.2.
- **Radial velocity (51 Peg)**: fold a published RV series and read K → M sin i. It gives chapter 3 the slides' first method and connects to 1.6's binaries.
- **Planet densities**: with 3.1's unlock (B7), plot *Planet radius* against *mass* (measured masses only, B8) with lines of constant density. Rock, water and gas separate, and Teq shows the inflated hot Jupiters. Data: the shipped exoplanets.csv.
- **MACHO test (Lecture 4)**: from the counter above, estimate τ and the event count toward the LMC and compare with what OGLE/EROS saw. Data: published event rates; no new photometry needed.
- **HII regions and the ISM (Lecture 3 §3.2–3.3)**: the SDSS spectra already shipped for chapter 9 (zspec, bpt) show Balmer and [O III] lines. A short mission "why is the Orion Nebula green" (forbidden lines, the density argument) would cover a whole section of Lecture 3 that the game skips.
