# Chapter 1 (Lecture 1, missions 1.1–1.8): audit

## Summary
- All eight missions were played through the real UI (`H.open({before:id})`, menus, box drags, handle drags, sliders, buttons). Seven can be finished; **1.3 cannot**: its task names "Absolute magnitude M_G", locked until 1.3 itself is done. Because `invsq` is never unlocked, 1.5, 1.8, 2.1 and 2.3 (which need it) stay blocked too.
- Worst after that: 1.3's "smear → bands" premise is false on screen (the 100 pc sample is already an HR diagram in apparent G); 1.1's target stars (V < 1, Sirius, Rigel) are clipped out of the chart by the autoscale; white-dwarf temperatures are capped at 11,590 K; switching dataset during 1.8 makes the result claim 137 pc and 78 pc "agree".
- Lectures are accurate (every worked example and practice answer re-checked) but miss slide topics: R ∝ T² along the main sequence, luminosity classes, the HR diagram first drawn for clusters, α ≈ 3 / 5 for L ∝ M^α, the EM spectrum and atmospheric windows.
- Top 3 improvements: (1) unlock a mission's own concept when its exercise starts; (2) rebuild 1.3 on the naked-eye sky (V vs B−V is a real smear; M_V shows the bands), with a "move every star to 10 pc" slider; (3) fix the autoscale clipping and the instrument-mode problems (no legend, chart resizing on hover, controls below the fold at 1100×800).

- Exercise: finishable through the UI, but see B3 (target stars clipped) and B9 (sky stays on a 100° dome). Threshold (8–30 stars, all V < 1.6) is fair once the stars are visible.

- Views: the magnitude histogram's x axis runs 7 → 1 (reversed), so "each magnitude fainter holds 3× more" reads right-to-left; the drop at 6.5 is the catalogue cut, not the sky (caption does not say so).

### 1.2 A year-long wobble (parallax)

### 1.3 Correct for distance (hrd)

## Gaps against the slides
Lecture 1 slides (Hebrew text in `sources/Lecture1.txt`); Tutorials 1–2 are OCR of handwriting and unreadable beyond their topics (intensity, solid angle and flux, Wien and Stefan–Boltzmann derivations; binaries and scaling relations).
- **HR diagram (§1.5)**: slides say Hertzsprung and Russell drew it independently around 1911 and **first for a cluster** (one distance, so flux ∝ L). The game says 1905/1913 and never makes the cluster point; 1.8 could close with it.
- **Main sequence R ∝ T²** (slides: L ∝ T⁸ approximately; smallest stars ½ T☉ → ¼ R☉, largest 5 T☉ → 25 R☉; giants up to ~1 AU, white dwarfs ~10⁴ km). Missing; 1.5 has the data and the radius lines to show it.
- **Mass–luminosity**: slides α ≈ 3 (M > M☉) and ≈ 5 (small stars); main sequence spans 0.1–100 M☉; white dwarfs ≈ 0.6, ≤ 1.4 M☉; "the main sequence was first thought a cooling sequence, it is a mass sequence". Only partly in 1.7.
- **Spectral classification**: luminosity classes (G2**V**, Roman numerals for giants) and the L, T classes are missing from 1.4's spectral-sequence step.
- **Other measurements from spectra** (slide "לא נתעמק"): rotation from line width, inflow/outflow from line shape, magnetic field from the Zeeman effect. Missing.
- **Light (§1.2)**: Maxwell's equations, the four properties of an EM wave, the EM spectrum and the atmospheric window (and why eyes see "visible"). Missing in chapter 1 (the *Light* menu exists but is not used here).
- **Distance from a blackbody fit** (slide "point to ponder": F/πB = (R/d)², so a radius or a calibration is needed). Missing; a natural bridge 1.4 → 1.5.
- **Binaries**: astrometric binaries and the SS Leporis interferometric image (slides) are missing; the slides' circular-orbit derivation is covered.
- **History (§1.1)**: Ptolemy's epicycles, precession (26,000 yr), Lippershey 1608. Covered only in summary form; fine for the game.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
- **"Move every star to 10 pc" slider (1.3)**: on the naked-eye sky (B−V vs V), a slider t from 0 to 1 draws each star at V − t·(m − M). The cloud contracts into the main sequence and the giant branch as the student drags. Fixes the belief that apparent brightness says what a star is. Easy: a pseudo-dataset like `INS.slideRef` (instruments.js) with per-point shifts.
- **Planck window on a log λ axis (1.4)**: 100 nm–10 μm, with the two limits and a side plot of the BP/RP ratio against T that flattens at the Rayleigh–Jeans ceiling (HW1 Q2b). Fixes "any two colours give T". Easy: `INS.spectrograph` (instruments.js:64–110).
- **Parallax with the Earth's orbit inset and an ecliptic-latitude slider (1.2)**: the Earth's position beside the star's offset, and β from 0° (a line) to 90° (a circle). Fixes "the shift is always 2p". Easy–medium: `INS.parallax` (instruments.js:113–175) already has `A.parallaxShift`.
- **Orbit + velocity + eclipse (1.6)**: the `orbit2` explainer linked to the real velocity curves, an inclination slider that shrinks K by sin i and the masses by sin³ i, and a model eclipse light curve that disappears below i ≈ 80°. Fixes "why eclipses matter". Medium: explainers.js + `INS.binary`.
- **A star you drag on the L–T chart (1.5)**: a draggable point that shows the star's disc to scale next to the Sun, the Earth, Sirius B and Betelgeuse. Gives orders of magnitude for "the diagram is also a size chart". Medium: chart `point` overlay with `onmove` (chart.js) and a small canvas.
- **Proper-motion vectors on the sky (1.8)**: draw μ as arrows on the dome for the Pleiades field and the Hyades, so members visibly drift together and the Hyades converge. Medium: sky.js markers.
