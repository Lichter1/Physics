# Chapter 5 (Island universes: redblue 5.1, zwicky 5.2, tully 5.3): audit

## Summary
- All three missions can be finished through the UI alone after the earlier missions (`H.open({before: id})`): no locked or missing axis, dataset, colour-by option or tool. Chapter 5 has no `needs` and no `unlocks`, so the locked-axis bug cannot happen here. The flip side is that the chapter adds nothing to the Notebook.
- Worst problem: 5.3's done window (slope 3.0 to 4.6) rejects the data's own best lines. The bisector slope is 4.57, the orthogonal slope 4.78, and a line with slope 4.63 and scatter 0.32 dex stays "in progress", while slope 3.10 with scatter 0.43 dex passes. The status gives no hint why.
- Other bugs: Next stays enabled after the student undoes the measurement, and the result then mislabels it. Switching dataset in 5.3 throws a TypeError every 400 ms and loses the line. The 5.1 "Colour by shape" instruction does nothing while the selection is on. The Tully–Fisher explainer shows the mass ×8 where it should be ×16. Olbers' worked example uses galaxy discs, not stars (unlike HW5 Q5 and the slides).
- Top 3 improvements: (1) fix the 5.3 threshold and add a baryonic-mass axis so the "tighter BTFR" claim can be tested; (2) give 5.2 an M/L number (≈200, as in the slides) and a crossing-time step, and let the student choose R; (3) add the slides' missing quantitative arguments (AGN efficiency, galaxy-collision rate, cluster gas mass, the uranium clock) as worked examples, plus a 21-cm line-profile explainer.

## Mission by mission

### 5.1 Red and blue galaxies (redblue)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes.** Dataset "Nearby galaxies", axes M_r × u−r and colour-by "Shape (Galaxy Zoo volunteers)" are all open at `before:'redblue'`; nothing in the task, lecture or explanation is locked. A real drag box (M_r −23.3…−18, u−r 2.4…3.25) gives "done 2964 selected, median 2.56". Result: 33% elliptical and 17% spiral among red, against 4% and 41% among the others. All 3 practice answers verified (2.512, 346 AU, 7.94×10⁷ M☉).
- **Lecture** (8 beats; images Antennae and M87 jet load; `colourmix` explainer and Schechter live chart render; no TeX errors): accurate and readable. M87, then M101, then *colour is age* builds the right intuition. The Schechter chart with the survey limit (−18.9 at z = 0.05, checked) is a good selection-effect lesson. Checked arithmetic: star-collision odds 6×10⁻¹², quasar size 173 AU, Eddington mass 8×10⁸ M☉, 3C 273 at ≈680 Mpc. Missing: a picture of Hubble's tuning fork (the beat says "tuning fork" but shows only M87). The AGN beat is long (about 1,600 characters); split it into "what an AGN is" and "how big and how heavy".
- **Exercise**: the prediction is good but easy (most students know ellipticals are red). A sharper question: "Are the *brightest* galaxies mostly red or blue?" or "Is a red galaxy always an elliptical?" (33% are, 17% are spirals). The result answers "what shape are red galaxies" while the question asks "what colour are ellipticals". Add "median u−r: ellipticals 2.6, spirals 1.8" (computable from `morph`). The 80% vote cut leaves 54% "unsure", which dilutes every percentage; say so or use the majority vote. B8: too easy.
- **Views**: points in one amber colour. Colouring them by their own u−r (blue to red) would make the bimodality visible before any selection. The sky shows no galaxies until something is selected (B7); after selection the SDSS footprint lights up, which is nice.

### 5.2 Weighing a cluster of galaxies (zwicky)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes.** Dataset "Toward the Coma cluster", cz histogram, nothing locked. Drag 4,500–9,500 km/s: "done 1022 galaxies, mean cz 6948, σ = 897". Result: d = 99.3 Mpc, rms R = 2.33 Mpc, M = 1.3×10¹⁵ M☉, stars 3.1×10¹³ M☉ (42× less). Physically right. Practice answers verified (1.05×10¹⁵ M☉, 16.4 Mpc). A box over the core only (6,000–8,000) gives σ = 534 and stays "in progress" with no hint that the wings were cut off; add one.
- Leaving half-way (clicking 5.3 in the list, then 5.2 again) returns to the lecture step but keeps the 1,022 selected galaxies, harmless. Choosing "Nearby galaxies" mid-mission and boxing its cz spike gives σ = 536 with no hint that it is the wrong dataset.
- **Lecture** (8 beats; Abell 370, Webb deep field and DF2 images load; `virial` explainer runs): good, and gives the lecturer's lensing field its place. θ_E = 35″ and the DF2 virial mass 1.5×10⁸ M☉ check out. Against the slides: the X-ray gas is "several times the mass of the stars" (the slides say about **10×**, at T ≈ 10 keV); missing are the crossing time R/σ ≈ 1 Gyr ("clusters are bound and old"), the baryon fraction ≈ 15%, the M/L ≈ 200–300 number, and the hydrostatic X-ray mass. The `virial` explainer could plot the line-of-sight speed histogram of its own 40 galaxies, so the student sees σ appear.
- **Exercise**: the prediction (100 / 1,000 / 10,000 km/s) is excellent: it separates the internal dispersion from the 7,000 km/s recession. The result should print **M/L_r = M_vir / L_r ≈ 210**, to connect with the "hundreds of times" in the lecture and the slides' 200–300. R is set by the 5° field cut, not by the student. Let them box a radius on a `sepc` histogram, or show how M changes with R.
- **Views**: B7 (no galaxies until selected). Once the spike is selected, the cluster lights up on the sky, which is the best moment of the chapter. Show the unselected foreground and background galaxies too, for contrast.

### 5.3 Spin tells power (tully)
- **Finishable through the UI: yes, but only for lines with slope ≤ 4.6** (B1). Axes Vflat × L36 open, the filter applied (129 of 175 galaxies), the line handles at (60 km/s, 0.5) and (250 km/s, 3) are visible and draggable. Real drags to slope 4.57 → done. Result: "L ∝ V^4.57; at 200 km/s about 93×10⁹ L☉". Practice answers verified (16, 13.39 Gyr).
- **Lecture** (8 beats; `tf` and `ladder` explainers render): the derivation L ∝ V⁴/(G²Σ) is correct, but the explainer's mass bar is wrong (B5). The ladder beat (TRGB, masers at 7.6 Mpc, SNe Ia, fundamental plane) and the Hubble-time beat (13.97 Gyr for H₀ = 70) are correct. Olbers (B6) mismatches the homework. The 21-cm beat has the equation W ≈ 2V sin i but no picture of the double-horned profile, which is the key visual. Two beats (Olbers, the age of the Universe) are cosmology, not Tully–Fisher; they would sit better as a short separate mission or as the opening of chapter 6.
- **Exercise**: the explanation says the relation is "tighter still when the gas mass is added", but there is no baryonic-mass axis to test it. SPARC already has L36 and MHI: add `Mbar = 0.5·L36 + 1.33·MHI`. The result ends with "measure its flux and you have its distance" but never does it. Add a final step: pick a SPARC galaxy, predict L from its Vflat, and compare the resulting distance with SPARC's D. The lecture says the calibration comes from Cepheids, yet the mission has no `needs: ['leavitt']`. Narratively it should.
- **Views**: dome on NGC 3198 (good), all-sky dome at fov 140 for Olbers (fine).

### Unlock chain (whole course)
- Chapter 5 missions declare no `needs` and no `unlocks`. The galaxies dataset offers d = cz/H₀ and M_r with no `needs`, while chapter 6 makes "Redshift gives distance and age" (`cosmo`, from 6.1) a concept to unlock. This is not a bug, and gating M_r would *create* a locked-axis bug in 5.1, but the chapter breaks the game's "each mission unlocks a quantity" promise. Option: 5.2 unlocks `virial` (cluster mass, M/L columns in `coma`), 5.3 unlocks `tf` (a Tully–Fisher distance column in `sparc`, comparable with SPARC's D).

## Gaps against the slides
(Lecture 5 slides; HW5 Q1–Q6. Tutorials 5–6 are handwritten-note OCR and unreadable, apart from "luminosity function", "AGN", "mean free path".)
- **Galaxy–galaxy collision rate** (slides: R = 50 kpc, v = 500 km/s, n = 0.01 Mpc⁻³ → 1 in 500 galaxies in 10¹⁰ yr; 10⁴× denser in clusters → a few Gyr, hence ellipticals in clusters). The game has only the star–star version.
- **AGN energetics**: a jet a Mpc long means years of activity; with efficiency 0.007 (fusion), 10¹⁰ M☉ of fuel is needed, against 0.057 (accretion) and 10⁹ M☉. AGN types (Seyfert, radio galaxy, BL Lac, quasar) and the **unification model**; broad lines of 10⁴ km/s. The Fermi bubbles and the G2 cloud are also missing.
- **Clusters**: crossing time; the Butcher–Oemler effect; ICM ≈ 10× stellar mass at 10 keV, bremsstrahlung; hydrostatic mass; baryon fraction ≈ 15%; lensing mass (a cylinder) against virial mass (a sphere); the cosmic web and homogeneity above 100 Mpc (Millennium simulation); the Genzel et al. 2017 falling rotation curves at high z.
- **Distance ladder**: SN 1987A light-echo ring (0.85″ → LMC distance), eclipsing binaries in the LMC, GCLF/PNLF peaks, **surface-brightness fluctuations** (Poisson √N), the Phillips Δm₁₅ relation for SNe Ia, the **Sunyaev–Zel'dovich** y parameter + X-ray distance. Only TRGB, masers, SNe Ia and the fundamental plane are named.
- **Hubble law**: the slides' "no centre" argument (v₁ − v₂ = H₀(d₁ − d₂)), and the **uranium clock** (²³⁵U/²³⁸U → 6.2 Gyr lower limit, ≈10 Gyr with several generations). The game states "thorium and uranium… about 13 Gyr" without the calculation.
- **HW5 Q1** (why other galaxies are external: Cepheids, M31's 41 kpc size, v > v_esc, the zone of avoidance) is not used, though the Great Debate is chapter 4's subject.
- **HW5 Q6** (testing a new standard candle with a same-distance sample) would suit the tully exercise.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
- **21-cm line profile** (new explainer in explainers.js, like `tf`): a rotating disc with sliders for V_flat and inclination i. The student sees the double-horned HI profile widen and narrow with sin i, and the observed W/2 matches V only edge-on. It fixes the idea that "line width = speed" whatever the tilt. Easy: about 60 lines, a canvas and two sliders.
- **Galaxy colour from a star-formation history** (extend `colourmix`): sliders for "star formation stops at t" and "burst at t"; plot a track on the actual M_r–u−r lens (as a moving marker over the 14,000 SDSS points). It shows the green valley crossing time. Medium difficulty: a chart overlay marker from instruments.js.
- **Virial explainer with a measured σ**: under the simulated cluster, a live histogram of line-of-sight speeds, the σ it gives, and the mass 3σ²R/G against the true mass. It answers "why does a dispersion weigh a cluster". Easy, in explainers.js:490.
- **Choose R in 5.2**: a second step with a `sepc` histogram and a draggable vline for R, M(<R) updating. It shows that M grows with R (as a dark halo does). Easy: reuse the vline instrument.
- **Baryonic Tully–Fisher toggle in 5.3**: add an `Mbar` variable (and a Υ slider) so the student sees the scatter shrink and the slope approach 4, the MOND prediction from mission 4.3. Easy: catalog.js:387.
- **Faber–Jackson mini-task**: the galaxies dataset already has `sigma`. Filter `morph = elliptical` and fit L ∝ σ^a (expected a ≈ 4). It reuses the line instrument.

## Suggestions: new missions or exercises
- **Faber–Jackson** (L5 scaling relations): SDSS `galaxies` (shipped, has `sigma` and Galaxy Zoo shape). Select ellipticals, fit L ∝ σ⁴, compare the scatter with Tully–Fisher.
- **Schechter fit** (HW5 Q3): an exercise version of the lecture chart. Drag M* and α (sliders, the `model` instrument with χ²) on the M_r histogram, above the survey limit only. Shipped data.
- **A Tully–Fisher distance** (L5 ladder, HW5 Q6): calibrate on the SPARC galaxies with Cepheid or TRGB distances (SPARC's distance method column, if kept by the pipeline; not verified), then predict D for the rest and plot D_TF against D_Hubble.
- **Clusters by their X-ray gas** (L5 ICM, HW5 Q4): MCXC or eROSITA eRASS1 (already in DESIGN.md as "to test"). Gas mass against galaxy mass → baryon fraction ≈ 15%.
- **Olbers / the dark sky** (HW5 Q5): a slider version of the HW solution: age of the Universe against the fraction of the sky covered by stellar discs (1 − e^{−ct/ℓ}).
