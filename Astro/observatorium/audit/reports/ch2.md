# Chapter 2 (Lives of stars): audit

## Summary
All four missions can be finished through the real UI after chapter 1 (`H.open({before: id})`): every axis, colour option and dataset they name is unlocked; nothing in chapter 2 asks for a locked variable, and no console errors appeared. The order of `needs`/`unlocks` is consistent (2.3 needs 2.2), but the `leavitt` concept unlocks no variable at all (B10).
Worst problems: 2.2 Leavitt is hard to finish with a mouse (fold window ±0.002 d vs a 0.2 d-per-pixel slider and a ±0.05 d fine tune, B5), its stage 2 keeps the stage-1 fold controls that can fold the P–L diagram (B8) and its line handles jump away on hover (B9, affects every hand-fitted line in the game); in 2.3 the Milky Way Cepheids to slide are off-screen at the start and then hidden under the LMC points (B11); the 2.1 turn-off explainer shows nothing below 0.8 Gyr (B3).
Top 3 improvements: (1) fix the fold controls (wider fine tune, clear tools at stage 2, stage-aware task text) and the hover resize; (2) make 2.3's reference population visible and use the student's own Leavitt slope there; (3) add the slide topics the chapter skips with a live tool each: an HR evolutionary-track/isochrone explainer, the Crab's minimum-density argument, the P–Ṗ death line and magnetars, and the SN 1987A ring distance to the LMC.

## Mission by mission

Played through the real UI with `H.open({before: id})` at 1600×950 (scripts `work/ch2/t_*.js`, results `work/ch2/*.json`); layouts checked at 1100×800 and 390×844 (`layout-*.json`, `shots/ch2/layout-*`). All four reach "done" and the Explain step with correct verdicts; all practice answers are accepted and their arithmetic is right.

### 2.1 Clocks in clusters (turnoff)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes. Axes `bp_rp0` and `MGc` (need parallax + invsq, from 1.2/1.3) and colour-by "Cluster" are unlocked; the mission's own `needs: [parallax, invsq, masslum]` are all met after chapter 1. Dragging the four rules by mouse to −1.0 / 0.6 / 2.7 / 3.6 gives "4 of 4" (shots `turnoff-ex0/ex1`). Rule colours match the legend. Works at 1100 and 390 px.
- **Lecture** (7 beats): physics and numbers checked (pp vs CNO T⁴/T¹⁷, Sun 10 Gyr vs Kelvin–Helmholtz 31 Myr, SN 1987A's 24 neutrinos): all correct. Clear and not too wordy. The only live tool, the turn-off explainer, is broken for young ages (B3). Beat 1 "How a star ages" is a dense paragraph with no picture: the slides' evolutionary tracks (0.6–5 M☉) are the natural figure.
- **Exercise**: task clear; thresholds fair (windows ±0.7–2 mag around the real turn-offs). Pleiades counts as placed from the start (B1). The sky stays on SN 1987A (B2). The result table (M_G → L → M → age, against model ages) is excellent: it shows the simple clock failing for old clusters, and the Explain says why (evolved turn-off stars, metal-poor stars).
- **Prediction** ("main sequence stops lower down") is a good one but easy; a sharper misconception: "an old cluster has *more* red stars because its stars have cooled" (the main sequence itself does not move).
- **Explain & practice**: one practice problem (2 M☉ → 1.77 Gyr, correct). Add the lecturer's own scalings (L ∝ M⁴: 10 M☉ lives 1/1000 of the Sun) and HW3 Q1b (turn-off mass at 1 Gyr = 2.5 M☉).
- **Views**: the Pleiades, M67 and NGC 6397 photos appear in the lecture cues; during the exercise no cluster is on the dome.

### 2.2 Leavitt's law (leavitt)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes, but with difficulty. Datasets `cep_lc` and `cep_ogle` need no concept. Stage 1 accepts 11.8585–11.8625 d only; the trial-period slider moves ~0.2 d per pixel and fine tune covers ±0.05 d, so only two coarse positions work (B5). In my run a precisely aimed mouse drag (11.841 d) plus fine tune +0.020 d passed; a student usually needs the arrow keys. Stage 2: dragging "your line" fails on the first press because the hover readout resizes the chart (B9); re-aiming works: slope −3.10, scatter 0.24 mag → done.
- **Leaving and switching**: stage 2 keeps the fold controls and the "Fold" box folds the P–L chart (B8); the task text stays on stage 1 (B7); fine tune is discarded by the coarse slider (B6).
- **Lecture** (4 beats + summary): correct (1,777 variables in 1908, 25 SMC Cepheids in 1912, δ Cep 5.37 d, κ-mechanism, P ∝ (Gρ)^−½). The Cepheid explainer works; at periods > 36 d its 6 s trace never shows a full cycle (ok to leave). Missing: Type I vs Type II Cepheids and RR Lyrae (slides 2.5.3) are only mentioned in 2.3's history.
- **Exercise**: two stages that mirror Leavitt's own work; the "scatter N× the sharpest" readout is good feedback. Prediction (brighter pulse more slowly) targets a real misconception. Result numbers right (30 d vs 1 d: 4.6 mag = 68×).
- **Explain & practice**: practice (10-day Cepheid at V = 15 → 64.6 kpc) correct. Unlock message promises a calibration but no new variable appears (B10).

### 2.3 A yardstick for the cosmos (yardstick)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes. `W` and `P` need nothing; `MW` (absolute W) needs parallax + invsq, unlocked. Mouse drag of the μ slider to 18.60 → "on top", LMC at 52.5 kpc. The data's own best match is μ = 18.63 (16–84%: 18.55–18.73), i.e. 53.3 kpc; the window 18.25–18.95 (44.7–61.7 kpc) is generous but fine.
- **Exercise problems**: the 616 Milky Way Cepheids are invisible at the start and hidden at the answer, and they are green while the text says grey (B11). Nothing on screen shows the student's 2.2 slope.
- **Lecture** (5 beats): Wesenheit, A_G = 2.74 E(B−V), Hubble 1924 (factor ≥ 2 too close), Baade 1952, H₀ 73.0 ± 1.0 vs 67.4 ± 0.5 (8%): all correct. The ladder explainer is static and clips labels (B12). Missing from Lecture 5's ladder: the SN 1987A light-echo ring and detached eclipsing binaries as the two other routes to the LMC (Pietrzyński 2019 appears only in the result).
- **Prediction** (5 mag fainter → 10× farther) is a good inverse-square/log trap. Explain text says "a few percent beyond"; the data give ~7% (53 vs 49.6 kpc): say "about 7%".
- **Practice**: μ = 18.48 → 49.7 kpc, correct. Add: the student's own Leavitt line + this zero point → distance to an SMC Cepheid.

### 2.4 Lighthouses (pulsars)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes. P, Ṗ and colour "In a binary?" need nothing. A mouse box from (1.2 ms, 3×10⁻¹⁷) to (30 ms, 10⁻²²) selects 435 pulsars, median 4 ms → done; result "78% in binaries against 1% of ordinary pulsars, median age 4.7 Gyr" is right for the ATNF data. Works at 1100 and 390 px.
- **Lecture** (6 beats): numbers checked: nuclear density 4×10¹⁷ kg m⁻³, Sun → 0.64 ms, Crab Ė = 4.5×10³¹ W and τ = 1,260 yr, R_s = 29.5 km, M87* 128 AU and 15→40 μas, GRB E_iso = 1.2×10⁵¹ erg: all correct. Pictures (Pioneer plaque, M87*, Sgr A*) and the live Fermi T₉₀ histogram load. The lighthouse explainer gives two pulses per turn (B13). The black-hole and GRB beats are long; they fit the chapter but dilute the pulsar story.
- **Exercise**: clear; prediction (millisecond pulsars are old) is a strong misconception probe. Guide-line labels are hard to read (B14); no death line, no magnetars, though the dataset has a "Kind" colour with magnetars.
- **Practice**: τ for P = 1 s, Ṗ = 10⁻¹⁵ → 15.8 Myr, correct. Citation HW2 Q1 is wrong (B16).

## Gaps against the slides
Lecture 2 (stellar evolution, remnants, variables) and the ladder parts of Lectures 4–5, compared with the chapter-2 lectures (white dwarfs, Chandrasekhar and Roche lobes are in chapter 1 and not repeated here).
- **Mass limits of stars** (L2 §2.3): below 0.08 M☉ no hydrogen fusion (brown dwarfs, Jupiter); above ~60–100 M☉ radiation pressure (∝ T⁴) beats gas pressure (∝ T). Not mentioned.
- **The lecturer's scaling exercise**: L ∝ M⁴ → a 10 M☉ star is 10⁴ L☉ and lives 1/1000 as long (~10 Myr); 0.1, 10, 100 M☉ lifetimes and T_eff ≈ 5,800/√3 ≈ 3,300 K for 0.1 M☉; "after a long time, is a population red or blue?" Missing; the game uses only the student's slope.
- **Population III** (first stars, no metals): missing (I and II are covered).
- **Post-main-sequence stages in order**: subgiant, RGB (~10% of the MS life), helium flash at 10⁸ K and 10³ g cm⁻³, horizontal branch (<1% of life), AGB with two shells, thermal pulses and mass loss, CO white dwarf, "black dwarf". The game compresses this into one paragraph and has no track figure (the slides show tracks for 0.6, 1, 2 and 5 M☉).
- **Stellar-structure equations** (hydrostatic equilibrium, mass continuity, energy, radiative transport), qualitatively, and power-law scalings from them: missing (slides say "only mention").
- **Nucleosynthesis**: triple-alpha, fusion up to iron, s- and r-processes, heavy elements from supernovae and neutron-star mergers (periodic-table figure): only "onion ending in iron" is there.
- **Supernova energetics and light curves**: 1–10 M☉ ejected at 10⁴ km/s → 3×10⁵¹ erg, ~1% as light; type II vs Ia light curves and spectra. Types are named; energetics, light curves and spectra are missing.
- **Pulsars**: the minimum density from spin (gravity ≥ centrifugal: ρ ≥ 3π/GP² ≈ 1.3×10¹⁴ kg m⁻³ for the Crab), which rules out white dwarfs, is the slides' central calculation and is missing. Also the P–Ṗ "lives of pulsars" slide (born top left, move down-right, death line, graveyard, possible field decay), Voyager record, optical/X-ray pulsars.
- **Black holes**: gravitational time dilation and redshift formulas, light slowing to zero at R_s, the "frozen star" argument (photon rate ∝ e^(−t/τ)), Hawking radiation, no-hair theorem, Kerr/frame dragging: only one sentence ("clocks run slow, light is redshifted, tides stretch"). HW2 Q5 (b) tides and (c) redshift are not practised.
- **Other stars** (§2.5): Wolf–Rayet stars missing; RR Lyrae details (horizontal-branch, 0.2–2 d periods, ~40–50 L☉, PL relation in the infrared) and Type I vs Type II Cepheids only touched in passing.
- **Distance ladder** (L5): the LMC from the SN 1987A light-echo ring (0.85″ ring radius and the light delay) and from detached eclipsing binaries; Cepheids in open clusters as a calibrator. (TRGB, masers, Tully–Fisher, SNe Ia are in 5.3 according to DESIGN.md; surface-brightness fluctuations, luminosity-function peaks and the Sunyaev–Zel'dovich distance are in the slides and, as far as I saw, nowhere in the game: not verified outside chapter 2.)

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
1. **Isochrone on the real clusters** (2.1). In the exercise, an "Age" slider draws a model isochrone (or the scaled turn-off + giant branch from the fixed explainer) over the four Gaia clusters; the student matches each cluster by age instead of only placing a rule. Fixes "the whole sequence shifts or cools with age". Medium: a `model` overlay (`INS.model`, `src/js/instruments.js:233`) with tabulated MIST/PARSEC isochrones (a few kB of JSON per age).
2. **Evolutionary track player** (2.1, lecture beat 1). Pick 0.6, 1, 2, 5 or 15 M☉, press play: a dot walks the HR diagram (MS → subgiant → RGB → He flash → HB → AGB → white dwarf, or → supergiant → supernova) with a clock showing how long each phase lasts (MS 90%, RGB ~10%, HB <1%). Fixes "stars spend their lives moving around the diagram" and gives the slides' track figure life. Medium: new explainer in `explainers.js` from tabulated tracks.
3. **Mass sets the fate** (2.1 "How stars end"). One mass slider, 0.05–150 M☉: brown dwarf (<0.08), red dwarf, Sun-like → white dwarf, >8 → neutron star, >25 → black hole, >100 → unstable (radiation pressure); lifetime with the student's slope. Covers HW2 Q1 and the slides' mass limits. Easy: explainer with bands, like HW2's own interactive.
4. **Fold by ear, not by eye** (2.2). Add a periodogram button: scatter vs trial period over 1–40 d as a curve, with a draggable marker. The student sees the dip, aliases and why five years pin the period to 10⁻⁴. Fixes the fiddly sliders (B5) and teaches what folding measures. Easy: `INS.foldScatter` already exists; plot it as a pseudo-dataset.
5. **Is the Crab a white dwarf?** (2.4). Slider for the star's radius at fixed 1.4 M☉ and the Crab's 33 ms spin: the equator's speed vs escape speed; a white-dwarf-sized star flies apart, a 10 km one does not (ρ ≥ 3π/GP²). Fixes "pulsars could be any spinning star". Easy: explainer.
6. **A pulsar's life on the P–Ṗ chart** (2.4). Press "age": a newborn Crab-like dot moves along constant B, crosses age lines, reaches the death line; a "recycle" button makes a companion spin it up to the millisecond island. Colour option "Kind" to show magnetars. Easy–medium: animated `point` overlay on the existing chart plus a death-line curve (B/P² = 0.17×10¹² G s⁻², i.e. Ṗ ∝ P³).
7. **Distance from a light echo** (2.3). Drag the ring radius on the SN 1987A image (0.85″) and the echo delay (~240 d) → physical radius c·t → distance; compare with the Cepheid μ. Teaches an independent rung and why rungs must agree. Easy: image + two sliders (like the photometry instrument).
