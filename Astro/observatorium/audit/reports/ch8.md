# Chapter 8, "Every kind of light" (missions 8.1 bursts, 8.2 frb, 8.3 gw): audit

## Summary
- All three missions can be finished through the real UI after only the earlier missions (also at 1100×800 and 390×844): nothing they ask for is locked. The chapter has no `needs`/`unlocks`, and the lens menus show no locked option; the only gated variable, lookback time (`cosmo`, mission 6.1), is already open. No console errors. The seven *Light* maps line up correctly in the equatorial, Galactic and horizon frames and carry the right credits.
- Worst problems (3 major): (1) every mission turns the sky to l ≈ 96° rather than the Galactic centre, while the readout says "centre of the Galaxy in the middle"; 8.1 is about crowding toward that centre. (2) The whole-sky visible photo view reloads its close-ups in an endless loop, about 10 loads and full re-renders a second while idle. (3) 8.3 plots final mass against total mass on log axes, where the 4% effect cannot be seen, so the "no loss" line already runs through the dots.
- Top 3 improvements: centre every Galactic view on l = 0 and plot 8.3 as mass radiated against total mass; add a short-vs-long T₉₀ step to 8.1 (the slides' BATSE result, with the data already loaded); make the static "windows" and interferometer explainers respond to their controls (an altitude slider; fringes that actually change).
- Counts: 0 blocker, 3 major, 16 minor, 3 cosmetic.

## Mission by mission

### 8.1 Where do the bursts come from? (bursts)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes. With `H.open({before:'bursts'})`: lecture, prediction, then real clicks on "Compare with the pulsars" and "Back to the bursts", and a real drag over |sin b| < 0.17 on the histogram. That selects 792 bursts and the mission is done, at 1600×950, 1100×800 and 390×844. Nothing needed is locked: sin b and the histogram are always open. A wrong prediction gets "You predicted 'crowd into the plane too'. The data say: come equally from every direction", which is correct but does not say why it was tempting.
- **Lecture:** 9 beats. The sky steps through visible, radio, far IR, Hα, X-ray and gamma light, all Galactic and centred on l = 0, with the right credits. Physics and worked examples check out (8.8′, 27 μas, 97 μm, 9.3×10⁷ K, 23 h 56 min giving two hours a month). It reads well, but it is long: eight beats of instrument history before the mission's own idea appears. Beats 4, 5 and 7 overflow at 1600×950 and need scrolling. The UV beat shows the Hα map, which is honest (said in the text) but is not UV. The "windows" and interferometer explainers are static in effect (B8, B9).
- **Exercise:** the pulsar comparison is a strong contrast (pulsars: 75% within 10°, ⟨cos θ⟩ = 0.55; bursts: 18% and 0.001). One bar, sin b 0.8–0.9, has 276 bursts against about 222, next to a low bar of 188. It is a fluctuation, but students will ask about it; √N error bars and the flat 17% expectation line would answer them. The threshold accepts |sin b| up to 0.22, which is fair for boxing whole bars. On a phone the status line sits about 1,000 px above the chart, so the student does not see "done" appear.
- **Explain & practice:** the numbers are right; practice answers check (8.81′, 26.8 μas, 58.0×10⁶ K). The isotropy error is overstated (B10) and the reasoning is circular (B16). The lecture never gets to the slides' other BATSE result, short against long bursts, though `grbs` has T₉₀ and a "short or long" colour.
- **Views:** sky centred wrong (B1); the Light menu says "Gamma rays" over the Drawn sky (B11); the scale ladder puts the bursts at "Galaxies" (B18).

### 8.2 Weighing the missing baryons (frb)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes, with the two sliders alone: clicking Ω_b at about 0.049 gives "done Ω_b = 0.048, χ²/N = 1.98", and an offset of 250 gives 1.55. A scan puts the best fit at Ω_b = 0.047, offset 252, χ²/N = 1.55. With the default offset of 150 the target needs Ω_b ≥ 0.048, and the status never says the goal is χ²/N < 2 (Ω_b = 0.044 shows "in progress … 2.15" with no hint). Nothing is locked.
- **Lecture:** the numbers check (DM 100 → 1.95 s; 0.25 protons per m³; 220 and 205 pc/cm³; dmCosmic(1) = 945, so "≈ 935 z" is a fair rounding). The dispersion explainer is good and live; above DM ≈ 600 the sweep piles up at the 12 s edge. The DM–latitude figure is a good idea, but its caption names the wrong colour (B7). The mission's relation omits χ_e, which the lecture includes.
- **Exercise & explain:** the scatter is real and the task says so. A dashed "no intergalactic gas" curve (Ω_b = 0, as 8.3 draws "no loss") would show what the fit buys. The slider label is misleading (B15). Practice 9.73 s and 280.5 pc/cm³ check.
- **Views:** radio sky, but centred on l = 96° (B1); the model label is cut off (B17).

### 8.3 Hearing black holes merge (gw)
- **Can it be finished?** Yes (ε = 4.2% → done; the window is 3.0–5.5% with χ²/N < 1). But the plot hides the effect (B6), a good-looking χ²/N outside the window gives no hint (B5), and the result can be reached with ε = 0 (B4). Nothing is locked.
- **Lecture:** the strain example checks (h ≈ 1.8×10⁻²¹, ΔL ≈ 7×10⁻¹⁸ m), as do H₀ = 3000/43 = 70 and the chirp-mass practice (1.219 M☉). The mass histogram shows the pile-ups and the gap well. GW150914's masses differ between the lecture, the brief and the data (B14). The chirp explainer works but is silent: a chirp you can hear is the obvious upgrade.
- **Explain:** "most efficient process known (fusion releases 0.7%)" is a good comparison. The peak power of 4×10⁴⁹ W is a constant, not computed (B4). The mass-radiated axis has negative values for three events (B13).
- **Views:** the sky shows visible light centred on Cygnus and has nothing to do with the mission (the `gw` data have no positions). It could show GW170817's host, NGC 4993, in the dome. The whole-sky visible view triggers the reload loop (B2).

### The *Light* menu (all seven maps), checked in all three frames
- **Method** (`work/ch8/bands.js`): Photo; each band chosen from `#sky-band`; Whole sky in the Equatorial and Galactic frames, then Horizon at Mitzpe Ramon on 2026-07-15, 23:00, looking south. Markers were placed (JS, for diagnosis) at Cas A, Cyg A, Vela, Crab, Geminga, Sco X-1, M87, LMC, the Galactic centre and the north Galactic pole. Grids: `work/ch8/bands/grid-equ.png`, `grid-gal.png`, `grid-hor.png`; raw maps with an l/b grid: `work/ch8/bands_grid.png`.
- **Alignment is correct in all three frames.** Cas A and Cyg A sit on the bright radio sources; Vela is bright in radio, X-rays and gamma rays; Geminga and Crab in gamma rays; Sco X-1 and the Virgo cluster (M87) in X-rays; the LMC in HI, far IR, Hα and X-rays; the Galactic plane lies on the visible Milky Way. In the horizon view the plane runs from Cygnus to the Galactic centre in every band, and the radio North Polar Spur rises from the plane at l ≈ 30°. The orientation is right (north up, l increasing to the left; the pipeline flips FITS rows, fetch_universe.py:277).
- **Credits are right for every band**, e.g. "Radio 408 MHz: Haslam et al. 1982 … via NASA SkyView" and "X-rays 0.7-1.6 keV: ROSAT All-Sky Survey diffuse background, R6 band (Snowden et al. 1997)". Only the visible view's close-up credit is wrong (B3: Coma, and in the horizon view M101 and the OGLE bulge field, all credited to DSS2).
- **Gaps:** there is no ultraviolet and no near-infrared band, though the lecture's UV and IR beats discuss both; survey gaps are unexplained (B22); "0.7-1.6" should take an en dash. After the lecture the Light menu can disagree with the Drawn look (B11).

## Gaps against the slides
- **Lecture 10, radio:** the slides' sub-mm part (bolometers, ALMA with the HL Tauri disk), polarisation, scintillation, and plasma lensing and extreme scattering events get one clause or none. The slide list of radio sources (the Sun, Jupiter's bursts, Sgr A*, AGN jets, radio relics in merging clusters, supernova remnants) is only partly covered. The Lorimer burst, repeaters, FRB 180916's 16.35-day period, SGR 1935+2154, missing baryons, SZ, ISW and line-intensity mapping are all covered (the last three in one line each).
- **Lecture 10, infrared:** the near/mid/far division and which near-IR windows open from the ground; Piazzi Smyth's 1856 detection of the Moon; Akari, Euclid and Hubble's WFC3 deep fields; and SOFIA and balloons (Stratoscope). SOFIA is the natural example for "how high must you go", the core of the windows beat.
- **Lecture 11, ultraviolet:** the 100–4000 Å range, Hubble's UV, AstroSat and GALEX's star-formation history (the slides' Madau–Dickinson plot) are missing; IUE, FUSE and ULTRASAT are covered.
- **Lecture 11, X-rays:** soft against hard X-rays (the 0.1 nm boundary), Geiger counters and scintillators, the HEFT balloon, Hitomi's calm gas in Perseus, Athena, Cyg X-1 as the first black-hole candidate, Tycho's remnant and the Sun in NuSTAR. Clusters weighed by hydrostatic equilibrium appear only as one sentence in the example.
- **Lecture 11, gamma rays:** the emission mechanisms (annihilation, inverse Compton, radioactive decay); the discovery by satellites watching for nuclear tests (only in the mission brief); solar flares; resolution of about 6′ at GeV energies; the Crab at TeV in 1989; CGRO's four instruments (COMPTEL's ²⁶Al map, OSSE, EGRET's 271 sources); soft gamma repeaters; Swift's 1,000th burst. Above all, **BATSE's split of bursts into short (< 2 s) and long**, which the slides list as a main result and the data can show.
- **Lecture 11, gravitational waves:** the second source, an inflationary background tested by CMB B-modes; the history (initial LIGO 2002–2010 found nothing; advanced detectors from 2015); the LIGO–Virgo mass plot of the "stellar graveyard"; lensed gravitational waves (one line). Multi-messenger astronomy, the mass gap, Hulse–Taylor and IceCube are covered.
- **Auto-guiding** (start of Lecture 11) belongs to chapter 7; not checked here.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
1. **Windows with altitude** (extend `X.windows`, explainers2.js:301; easy). Add a slider: sea level, Mitzpe Ramon (0.9 km), Mauna Kea (4.2 km), SOFIA (12 km), a balloon (40 km), orbit. The grey area shrinks as the student climbs, and clicking a band sets the *Light* menu, so the sky shows that band. It fixes "space telescopes are only for sharper pictures": at most wavelengths the air is simply opaque.
2. **Fringes that change** (explainers2.js:216; easy). Plot the fringe pattern against angle on a fixed arcsecond scale, inside the single-dish beam envelope, and add a "place the dishes" toy on an Earth map that shows the synthesized beam. Students see that baseline sets resolution and dish size sets the field.
3. **De-disperse a burst** (a new instrument next to `INS.model`, instruments.js:244; medium). Show a waterfall (frequency against time) of a burst with noise; the student slides a trial DM until the sweep stands upright and the summed pulse peaks. This is how FRBs are found, and it gives each 8.2 point a measured DM rather than a given one.
4. **Toy populations for isotropy** (observing.js:243; medium). Buttons draw a disk population of chosen scale height, a halo of radius R, or a cosmological one on the sky and the histogram, with the same ⟨cos θ⟩ and plane-fraction readout. Students see why a halo of R ≳ 100 kpc also looks even, which is why the 1995 debate needed the brightness distribution and then afterglows.
5. **Hear the chirp** (explainers2.js:251; easy with Web Audio). Play the waveform for the chosen chirp mass, sped into the audible band for neutron stars. Overlay GW150914's whitened strain from GWOSC (CC BY 4.0).
6. **Swipe between two bands** on the sky (sky.js `_photo`; medium). Drag a divider to compare, say, visible against far IR over Orion or the Galactic centre: "infrared sees through dust" becomes something seen. A hover readout of l, b and the map value would help too.

## Suggestions: new missions or exercises
1. **"Two kinds of burst"** (BATSE, 1993), after 8.1, using data already loaded. Show a histogram of log T₉₀ for `grbs` (4,430 bursts). The student places a divider at the dip near 2 s and colours by "short or long" (746 short). Then link to GRB 170817A (T₉₀ ≈ 2 s) and GW170817 in 8.3: short bursts come from neutron-star mergers, long ones from collapsing massive stars.
2. **"Too many electrons for the Milky Way"** (Lorimer 2007), as a step before the 8.2 fit. Plot `frbs` and `pulsars` as DM against Galactic latitude (the lecture's figure, made live) with a slider for the Galaxy's contribution, about 30/sin|b| pc/cm³. The student finds that almost every burst exceeds it.
3. **"The mass gap"**: a histogram of m₂ (and m₁) from `gw`, coloured by kind. The student counts objects between 2.5 and 5 M☉ and compares the counts with X-ray-binary black holes (5–20 M☉). The data exist.
4. **"A distance from DM"**: `pulsars` has DM and a DM-based distance. Fitting distance against DM gives the mean electron density of the disk (about 0.03 cm⁻³, the number the lecture quotes). It is a natural lead-in to 8.2's intergalactic electrons.
5. **"Standard siren"**: a single-event exercise with GW170817 (d from the waves; v from NGC 4993), giving H₀ ≈ 70. Do not use the catalogue's `z` column for H₀: GWTC redshifts are derived from the distance with an assumed cosmology, so the result would be circular.
