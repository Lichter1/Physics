# Chapter 6 (The expanding Universe, Lecture 7): audit

Scripts: `work/ch6/` (Playwright runs through the real UI; Node recomputation of thresholds and lecture numbers). Screenshots: `shots/ch6/`.

## Summary
- All four missions can be finished through the UI after only the earlier missions (played with real slider clicks and arrow keys, wrong predictions included); none has `needs`, every axis they set is observed and open, no console errors at 1600, 1100 or 390 px. 6.1 unlocks lookback time correctly.
- Worst problem: the model-fit instrument (6.1–6.3) silently breaks when the student changes an axis or dataset (χ²/N jumps to 10⁴–10⁵, curves vanish or lie flat, recovery only by *Reset*), and *What does it mean?* stays enabled after progress regresses (B9, B10).
- Several texts contradict the screen or the physics: supernovae "on the sky" that are not there, a "black" curve that is blue, a BBN explainer that disagrees with itself, "done … use the fine tune", "older than a constant expansion rate", BAO Δz 0.06 (is 0.044), lookback time "in every dataset with redshifts".
- 6.4 measures only the quasar's redshift: the forest itself, the subject of its prediction, is never used.
- Top 3 improvements: (1) make the model instrument follow (or lock) the axes and show the Ωm–ΩΛ χ² map with the student's point (the code exists in `OBS.snChi2`/MCMC); (2) give 6.4 a second step: click dips for absorber redshifts and measure the mean transmitted flux, with a Gunn–Peterson slider; (3) make the CMB views teach: monopole → dipole → Galaxy → anisotropies in the *Light* menu with a μK colour bar, and a "ruler" circle on the CMB map that follows the Ω_total slider.

## Mission by mission

### 6.1 accel: Fainter than expected
- **Locks:** none. Axes z and Δμ are open; `tlb` (lookback time) is locked, unlocked by this mission, which is correct.
- **Thresholds** (recomputed with the mission's own χ², `work/ch6/accel_fit.js`): χ²_min = 671.5 (N = 1466, χ²/N = 0.46 with diagonal errors). Done needs Δχ² < 8, q₀ < −0.15, 0.1 < Ωm < 0.65. At the default H₀ = 73.0 the flat window is Ωm = 0.318–0.382; H₀ must stay within 72.54–73.46. Only 404 of 30,000 (Ωm, ΩΛ) cells at 0.01 resolution pass: a narrow valley.
- Empty universe (Ωm = ΩΛ = 0) is only Δχ² = 36.6 worse at its best H₀ (71.2); matter only Δχ² = 650.
- The readout shows χ²/N = 1.7 for matter-only at H₀ = 73 and 0.46 at best: a student taught "χ²/N ≈ 1 is good" may stop at 1.7.
- Result at the best fit: age 12.4 Gyr at H₀ = 73 (younger than the 13.8 the lecture quotes, and below the 12–13 Gyr globular clusters the lecture says ΛCDM rescues); 0.36 mag fainter than matter-only at z = 0.5 while the explanation says "0.2–0.3 magnitudes, some 25%".

- **Played through the UI** (`work/ch6/play_accel.js`, `play_accel2.js`): wrong prediction "brighter", Ωm slider 1.00 → 0.30 and ΩΛ 0 → 0.70 gives Δχ² = 18 (not done; the textbook 0.3/0.7 fails at H₀ = 73), 0.35/0.65 gives done (Δχ² = 1). Verdict, result and unlock ("Redshift gives distance and age") correct; Lookback time then opens in the menus. No console errors.
- Leaving half-way and coming back restarts the mission at the lecture (state not kept); *Skip to the prediction* gets back quickly.
- **Lecture:** five beats + summary; covers redshift and time dilation, D_L vs D_A, SNe Ia and Phillips relation, q₀, the 2011 Nobel. Numbers re-checked (`work/ch6/nums.js`): D_L(1) = 6,608 Mpc, D_A = 1,652, D_A max at z = 1.61 (1,748 Mpc), μ = 41.3 → 1.82 Gpc ≈ z 0.345, ages 9.31 / 13.47 Gyr: all right; only B3 is wrong. "13.8 with Planck's H₀ = 67.4" holds for Ωm = 0.315 (13.80), not the Ωm = 0.3 of the sentence (13.99).
- Missing from the slides: the transition from deceleration to acceleration (slides p12: "z ~ 1"; ΛCDM gives z = 0.63, 6.1 Gyr ago); the slides' point that the Hubble diagram now reaches z ≈ 2 where the Universe was a quarter of its age (p10).
- Practice: all three answers right (0.0999, 4,571 Mpc, −0.55).
- **Views:** the Universe rung lights up when the exercise opens supernovae (correct); the supernovae on the drawn all-sky map show the SDSS Stripe 82 line along the equator, a nice real-survey footprint the lecture never mentions.

### 6.2 cmb: The afterglow
- **Locks:** none (ν, λ, I_ν, residual all observed).
- **Thresholds** (`work/ch6/cmb_fit.js`): best T = 2.72501 K, χ²/N = 1.09; done (χ²/N < 3) for T = 2.72495–2.72508 K. The coarse slider moves in 0.002 K steps (2.724 or 2.726), so the fine tune is needed, as the task says. Played through the UI: coarse 2.724 (χ²/N = 438), fine +0.0010 → 2.7250, χ²/N = 1.19, done. Result text correct (160.2 GHz, 410 cm⁻³, 1 + z = 1101).
- "peak at 160.2 GHz (λ = 1.87 mm)" invites the classic error: the peak of B_λ is at 1.06 mm, not c/ν_peak. One sentence would fix it.
- **Lecture:** six beats; arithmetic checked: photon fraction above 13.6 eV at 5,800 K is 5×10⁻¹⁰ (one in two billion, right), ρ_c = 8.5×10⁻²⁷ kg m⁻³, η = 6.1×10⁻¹⁰, v = 370 km/s. Practice answers right (2,970 K, 160.2 GHz, 370 km/s).
- Beat 1 says "The dome shows the whole sky in microwaves, with the Milky Way removed: this is that glow". The map is the WMAP ILC **anisotropy** map: the 2.725 K glow and the dipole are subtracted and the contrast is ±200 μK. As written, a student will think the glow itself is blotchy. No colour bar on the sky map. Beat 6 then shows the same map again as a figure in a different colour table (jet vs red-blue): say they are the same data.
- **Views:** the *Light* menu switches to "Microwave: the CMB" automatically and the credit line names WMAP correctly.

### 6.3 peaks: The shape of space
- **Locks:** none (ℓ, θ, D_ℓ observed).
- **Thresholds** (`work/ch6/peaks_fit.js`): best Ω = 0.9995 (χ²/N = 0.96); done for Ω = 0.9965–1.0030; 0.99 → 17.4, 1.01 → 17.5, matching DESIGN.md. The coarse slider (0.0006 per step) reaches it alone; the fine tune is not needed. Played: wrong prediction "closed", slider to 1.000, done; verdict and result correct.
- The model only rescales the Planck best-fit template in ℓ by D_A(Ω)/D_A(1) with Ωm fixed at 0.315: fine for geometry, but at Ω = 0.8 the stretched damping tail sits above the data, which a student may read as "too much power" rather than "peaks shifted".
- Lecture vs exercise: the ruler beat gives θ = 0.6° for the sound horizon, the summary says "ℓ ≈ 180°/angular size", the peak at ℓ = 220 is "about 1°", and the result says 0.82°, "twice the size of the full Moon" (it is 1.6×). The slides (p49) say the spots are half the wavelength, "a little smaller than the full Moon". Pick one convention and say why the first peak (ℓ ≈ 220) is not at π/θ_s ≈ 300.
- "sound waves, at over half the speed of light": c/√3 = 0.58c early, 0.46c at recombination (R ≈ 0.6). Say "about half".
- Practice answers right (0.818°, 0.606°, 4.46°).

### 6.4 forest: A forest of clouds
- **Locks:** none (λ and flux observed; the *Object* subset menu offers the quasar).
- **Played through the UI** (`work/ch6/play_forest.js`): wrong prediction "smooth", coarse z slider 1.000 → 2.960 is already done (|Δz| < 0.01; step 0.004), fine +0.004 → 2.964 = SDSS 2.963975. Verdict and result correct (Lyα at 482 nm, absorbers z = 2.13–2.96, age 2.17 Gyr checked). No console errors. Switching *Object* to the star-forming galaxy mid-exercise keeps the quasar lines and "done"; harmless.
- **The exercise stops where the physics starts.** The prediction is about the forest, the brief says "then look at what happened to its light on the way", but the only measured quantity is the quasar's redshift (the same skill as 9.1). Nothing asks the student to use the forest: no absorber redshift, no count of lines, no flux decrement. See the suggestion below.
- Result: "Every dip between 380 and 482 nm is Lyα absorbed by a hydrogen cloud". Below 406 nm (Lyβ at z = 2.964) dips can also be Lyβ of higher-z clouds, and some are metal lines; say "most".
- Status text bug B13; labels B14.
- **Lecture (5 beats):** BBN (n/p freeze-out, Y = 2(n/p)/(1+n/p), worked example 1/7 → 0.25: right), baryon census (Ω_b h² 0.0224 CMB vs 0.0221 D/H, D/H = 2.5×10⁻⁵, a sixth of the matter, < 10% in stars: matches slides p70–72), quasars (peak at z ≈ 2, 10.5 Gyr ago, checked; z = 7 at 0.76 Gyr, checked), the forest with a live SDSS chart, lensed quasars (Refsdal, H₀ ≈ 73, Risaliti & Lusso: slides p80–83). All three practice answers right (0.25, 486.4 nm, 2.70).
- BBN explainer contradicts its own text (B2); its axes have no tick labels, so "neutrons per proton" cannot be read off.
- The lecture never shows **why** the forest is a forest and not a trough: neutral fraction 10⁻⁴ (slides p78) is stated but not connected to the Gunn–Peterson optical depth (τ ≈ 10⁵ x_HI at z ≈ 3), which the explanation then cites. One sentence plus the numbers would make the prediction's third choice ("completely dark") a teachable wrong answer.
- **Views:** beat 3 swings the dome to 3C 273 in photographs (marker right, Virgo field with M87 and Abell 1689 close-ups); beat 1 shows the CMB map for BBN, which is unrelated to the text. The exercise marks "the quasar" on the visible all-sky photo (RA 207°, Dec +28°), correct.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
- **Ωm–ΩΛ plane next to the Hubble diagram (6.1).** The student's (Ωm, ΩΛ) is a dot on the χ² map with the flat line, the q₀ = 0 line and the no-Big-Bang corner; the dot moves with the sliders. Shows the degeneracy (supernovae alone fix Ωm − ΩΛ-ish, not each) and why 6.3 is needed. Easy: `OBS.snChi2` and the MCMC map drawing in `observing.js:257-300` already exist; show it in `imgview` or a second canvas in `#tools`.
- **Model on any axis (6.1–6.3).** Let the student fit on μ vs z (the Hubble diagram of the slides), on ν or λ, on ℓ or θ: give `INS.model` a transform per axis id. Fixes B9 and teaches that B_ν vs λ peaks at 1.87 mm while B_λ peaks at 1.06 mm.
- **q(z) strip (6.1).** Under the lens, q(z) = [Ωm(1+z)³/2 − ΩΛ]/E² for the student's universe, with the zero crossing labelled (z = 0.63 for Planck). Small addition in `missions.js` `describe`.
- **The CMB, layer by layer (6.2).** *Light* gains "CMB: uniform 2.725 K", "dipole (×1000 contrast)" computed analytically (3.36 mK toward l = 264°, b = 48°), "with the Galaxy" (WMAP W band from SkyView) and the existing ILC map, plus a μK colour bar and hover readout. The student sees why COBE's three panels look the way they do and measures v = cΔT/T themselves. Moderate: `sky.js` bands are image files; the dipole can be a computed band.
- **Redshifting a blackbody (6.2).** Slider z from 1100 to 0: the 3,000 K Planck curve slides and shrinks onto B_ν(T₀(1+z)) exactly; put Penzias and Wilson's single 4.08 GHz point on a log-ν FIRAS plot to show the Rayleigh–Jeans tail. Easy (new explainer in `explainers2.js`; log scale on `cmbspec.f`).
- **A ruler on the CMB map (6.3).** A circle of diameter 180°/ℓ₁ on the CMB sky that grows or shrinks with the Ω_total slider, beside the closed/flat/open triangle diagram. Easy: sky markers already take a radius (`sky.js:445`, `mk.r`).
- **Acoustic explainer that makes peaks (6.3).** Animate t (play button), and below it bars of each mode's amplitude² at t = 1, which become the peaks; a baryon-loading slider R shows odd peaks rising over even ones (the "what else the peaks weigh" beat). Moderate (`explainers2.js:76-93`).
- **Planck simulator lite (6.3, slides p53).** Sliders for Ω_b h² and Ω_c h² over a small precomputed grid of CAMB spectra (e.g. 5 × 5 × 2,500 values from `pipeline/`), interpolated. Moderate.
- **Forest to trough (6.4).** A neutral-fraction slider applies τ_GP ≈ 1.8×10⁵ x_HI ((1+z)/4)^1.5 to the forest band of the real spectrum: at 10⁻⁴ a forest, at 10⁻³ almost black, at 1 a Gunn–Peterson trough. Fixes "the IGM is mostly neutral". Easy on top of `INS.zfit`.
- **BBN with η (6.4).** Fix B2, add tick labels, and add an η slider with standard fitting formulas for Y_p and D/H (the "Schramm plot"), with the measured D/H band and the CMB value. Moderate.
- **Phone:** the exercise status (`#m-status`) is in the logbook pane far above the sliders at 390 px; mirror the done/in-progress pill next to the tools.
