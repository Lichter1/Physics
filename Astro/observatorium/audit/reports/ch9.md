# Chapter 9 "Reading galaxies" (missions 9.1 redshift, 9.2 bpt, 9.3 chain): audit

Played through the real UI with `H.open({before: id})` at 1600×950, 1100×800 and 390×844; scripts are in `work/ch9/`, screenshots in `shots/ch9/`. There were no console errors in any run. **Bugs: 0 blocker, 4 major, 11 minor, 5 cosmetic.**

## Summary
- **State:** all three missions can be finished through the UI after the earlier missions, and nothing they name is locked or missing. `chain`'s `needs: ['cosmo']` comes from 6.1, earlier in the course; "Hα luminosity" (needs `cosmo`) is open in 9.2. The physics, equations and every worked example and practice answer check out.
- **Worst problems:** the graders. In 9.1, sweeping the slider "measures" all three galaxies (B1). In 9.3, one click on "Run" finishes the mission with no tuning (B3). In 9.2, a correct near-vertical line scores 1% depending on which handle is on top (B2). In 9.3, a click on the χ² map shows 7.2's CCD image through a leaked click handler (B18).
- **Content:** the 9.3 lecture is all text (5 steps with no figure or explainer). The BPT step has no BPT figure. A vertical line passes 9.2, so [O III]/Hβ is never needed. Lecture 12's luminosity function, metallicity indicators and SFH fitting are missing.
- **Top 3 improvements:** (1) a χ²(z) strip under the redshift fitter, with a "record" button (fixes B1 and links to 9.3); (2) a trace plot with the burn-in marker, and a start step outside the good range (fixes B3 and teaches burn-in); (3) the Kauffmann and Kewley curves on the BPT, with composites in the score, plus a 1-D cost-function explainer for Lecture 13.

## Mission by mission

### 9.1 How far is that galaxy? (`redshift`)
- **Finishable through the UI:** yes. Dataset "Spectra for measuring redshifts", the three galaxy buttons and both sliders are all there; nothing is locked. It needs no concepts and unlocks none. The prediction (585 nm) and the result numbers are right: z = 0.0828 / 0.1038 / 0.4621 against SDSS 0.0830 / 0.1038 / 0.4617; d = 355 and 445 Mpc; lookback 4.7 Gyr. No console errors.
- **Lecture (7 steps):** good coverage of Lecture 12: the tuning fork, Sérsic, Tolman, scaling relations, M–σ, the red sequence and blue cloud, spectra, the 4000 Å and Balmer breaks, photometric redshifts, dropouts, Lyα emitters and deep fields. The arithmetic of every worked example and practice problem checks out (Tolman 3.0/6.0/4.77 mag; M–σ 1.8×10⁹ and 8.24×10⁸; [O III] z = 0.100; UDF 1.35×10⁷ fields, ~10¹¹ galaxies). It is dense: step 3 packs 6 ideas into one paragraph (scaling relations, M–σ, the luminosity function, morphology–density, the red sequence, quenching). The luminosity function gets half a sentence, with no Schechter form or figure. The template uses vacuum wavelengths (Hα 656.5, Ca H 397.0) while the text quotes air values (Ca H 396.8, [N II] 658.4) without saying why.
- **Exercise:** clear and real (true SDSS spectra), but graded too loosely (B1), and the match is judged only by eye. There is no "how close am I" feedback, and the fine-tune slider is never needed: the coarse step of 0.0006 is smaller than the 0.002 tolerance. The prediction is good: 400 nm catches "redshift doesn't move features". A choice of 446 nm (400 + 46) would catch the additive misconception better than 750.
- **Explain & practice:** good. It links the 4000 Å break to photometric redshifts. It could also say *why* the elliptical has no emission lines (no O/B stars, so no ionising photons): that sets up 9.2.
- **Views:** the sky marker follows each galaxy. The first lecture cue (drawn sky, ra 180, dec 30) shows naked-eye stars during a galaxy lecture; the SDSS close-ups on the *Photo* sky would fit better. Side paths: B8 and B9.

### 9.2 Young stars or a black hole? (`bpt`)
- **Finishable through the UI:** yes. Colour by "MPA-JHU classification" exists and is unlocked. The axes the explanation invites ("Stellar mass", "Star formation rate", "Gas metallicity") and "Hα luminosity" (needs `cosmo`, open) are all available. The handles drag with the mouse. A Kauffmann-like diagonal from (−0.45, 1.2) to (0.05, −1.2) scores 98–99%, and the starting line scores 82%.
- **Threshold:** too lenient on the physics. Composites are left out, so a **vertical** line at log [N II]/Hα ≈ −0.22 scores 99.4% (the best straight line scores 99.5%, my scan of `data/bpt.csv`), and [O III]/Hβ is not needed at all. The student never learns why the diagram is two-dimensional. Count composites as "not star-forming", or score against the Kauffmann curve. Then draw the Kauffmann (2003) and Kewley (2001) curves in Explain: the lecture gives the equation, but no chart draws it.
- **Lecture (6 steps):** the physics is right: Kennicutt 7.9×10⁻⁴², K–S index 1.4, the Kauffmann curve, MD14 numbers. The two live charts (main sequence; mass–metallicity) are good. But the **BPT step itself has no figure**: the key diagram first appears in the exercise. The mass–metallicity chart shows diagonal striping (gridded MPA-JHU O/H) without comment. "The history of star formation" has no chart. Metallicity indicators (Nagao: N2, O3N2, R23) and SFH parametrisation (Carnall) from the slides are missing.
- **Prediction:** weak. The lecture step just before states the answer ("boosting forbidden lines … relative to hydrogen's"), and so does the brief. Better: "A galaxy at (−0.2, −0.5) is…" (a composite), or "Which ratio rises if the gas is more metal-rich?"
- **Explain & practice:** fine; practice answers are correct (3.95; −1.14). Result wording: B16.
- **Views:** the sky shows the footprint of the 20,000 galaxies, a nice touch; a selection lights them up on the sky.

### 9.3 Let the chain find it (`chain`)
- **Finishable through the UI:** yes. `needs: ['cosmo']` is met after 6.1, and the order is consistent across the course. But it finishes with one click (B3). Both extremes behave as taught: step 0.4 gives 3% acceptance, step 0.003 gives 97%. Results: Ωm = 0.31 ± 0.07, ΩΛ = 0.59 ± 0.09, 100% accelerating, consistent with the grid best (0.31, 0.59). The chain caps itself at 20,000 steps. Leaving and coming back restarts cleanly.
- **Lecture (5 steps):** the content follows Lecture 13 closely and correctly: least squares; Δχ² = 1 and 2.3; bisection, simplex and gradient descent; local minima; 10³⁰ evaluations, 10²⁷ s = 3×10¹⁹ yr ≈ 2×10⁹ ages of the Universe ✔; Metropolis; burn-in; evidence; bootstrap; jackknife; simulate-and-recover; CNNs. But **none of the 5 steps has a figure, chart or explainer**: it is the only all-text lecture in the chapter, about the most visual algorithm in the course. The sky cue is the WMAP CMB, which has nothing to do with fitting supernovae; the Pantheon+ positions would be better.
- **Prediction:** 10²⁰ is right, but it is a mechanical copy of the worked example one step earlier (1000¹⁰). Better: "You make the step 10× larger. The acceptance… rises / falls / stays."
- **Explain & practice:** answers correct (30; 0.37; 1.5). B6 and B7 contradict the screen. There is no trace plot (parameter against step), which is how burn-in is actually chosen; the burn-in slider changes only the numbers, not the picture.

## Suggestions: simulations and interactive views
1. **χ²(z) strip under the redshift fitter (9.1).** Plot the template's χ² (or cross-correlation) against trial z with the student's z marked; there is a false dip for a wrong line identification (Hβ read as Hα). The student sees "matching" become a minimum, two missions before 9.3. Effort: moderate, a mini canvas in `INS.zfit` (src/js/observing.js:19) like `mcmc`'s `ins-mini`. It also fixes B1 ("record the minimum").
2. **Trace plot with the burn-in marker (9.3).** ΩΛ and Ωm against step number, with the burn-in as a vertical line; dim the dropped part of the trace on the map. The student sees the walk-in and then the "fuzzy caterpillar"; tiny steps give a slow random walk, huge steps give flat plateaus. This fixes "burn-in is an arbitrary number". Effort: small (observing.js:334, reuse `mini`).
3. **Cost-function explainer for Lecture 13.** A 1-D χ²(a) with two minima; buttons for a grid (dots), gradient descent (a ball that stops in the local minimum) and Metropolis (a walker that sometimes climbs out); a "dimensions" slider that counts grid points against the age of the Universe. This fixes "a minimiser always finds the best fit". Effort: small, a new `X.costfn` in src/js/explainers2.js, used in 9.3 steps 2–3.
4. **"Add the CMB" toggle on the χ² map (9.3).** A Gaussian prior on Ωm + ΩΛ = 1.00 ± 0.02: the long SN valley collapses to a spot, as the explanation says in words. Effort: small (one term in `chi`, observing.js:257).
5. **Bootstrap button (9.3).** Resample the 1,466 supernovae with replacement, refit on a coarse grid and histogram ΩΛ beside the chain's histogram: two methods, one error bar. Effort: moderate.
6. **Kauffmann and Kewley curves, and a "hardness" slider, on the BPT (9.2).** Draw both curves as `curve` overlays (supported by chart.js); a slider moves a model point from O-star to AGN ionisation up the right wing. This fixes "the AGN wing is just more star formation". The curves are trivial; the slider is schematic.
7. **Schechter fitter on the SDSS galaxies** with the existing `fit` instrument (φ*, M*, α on the M_r histogram): fills the largest Lecture 12 gap.
8. **Live cosmic SFH chart** (9.2 step 5) from MD14's formula ψ(z) = 0.015 (1+z)^2.7 / [1 + ((1+z)/2.9)^5.6] M☉ yr⁻¹ Mpc⁻³, with a lookback-time axis: a chart `fig`, no new data.
